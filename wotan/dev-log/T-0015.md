# T-0015: Add Moore-Penrose projector representations

**Status**: DONE | **Size**: L
**Phase**: -

## Context

T-0009 proves the intrinsic subspace identities and `Col(A Aᵀ) = Col(A)` without a
pseudoinverse. The paper also spells its projector matrices as `A A⁺`; mathlib 4.31
does not provide that finite Moore--Penrose layer.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [x] Define a finite real Moore--Penrose inverse from an orthogonal decomposition or
  singular-value decomposition, with all four Penrose equations.
- [x] Prove that `A A⁺` is the orthogonal projector onto `Col(A)` and that `A⁺ A` is
  the projector onto the row space.
- [x] Recover the paper's pseudoinverse matrix renderings of equations (12), (15)--
  (20), and (22) as corollaries of T-0009's intrinsic theorems.
- [x] Cover zero, rectangular, and rank-deficient matrices.
- [x] Build without `sorry` or custom axioms.

## Approach

- For a finite-dimensional real linear map `A : E →ₗ F`, restrict `A` to the
  orthogonal complement of its kernel and corestrict it to its range. Prove this
  restricted map bijective and invert it as a linear equivalence.
- Define the Moore--Penrose inverse by projecting onto `range A`, applying the
  restricted inverse, and including `ker(A)ᗮ` back into the domain. Prove the two
  product identities `A A⁺ = P_range(A)` and `A⁺ A = P_ker(A)ᗮ`; derive all four
  Penrose equations and uniqueness from these identities.
- Transport the construction through `Matrix.toEuclideanLin` for arbitrary finite
  rectangular real matrices. Prove the matrix Penrose equations, transpose symmetry,
  zero and rank-deficient behavior, and both canonical projector identities.
- Express equations (12), (15)--(20), and (22) as column-space corollaries by reusing
  T-0009's intrinsic subspace operations and the new canonical projector matrix.

## Implementation Notes

- Added `IPNPCNS/Subspace/MoorePenrose.lean`. For `A : E →ₗ F`, `effectiveMap A`
  restricts the domain to `ker(A)ᗮ` and the codomain to `range(A)`. The restriction is
  proved injective and surjective directly, without dimension-counting or a rank
  premise.
- Defined `moorePenroseInverse A` by range projection, inverse of the effective
  equivalence, and inclusion into `E`. Proved `A A⁺ = P_range(A)` and
  `A⁺ A = P_ker(A)ᗮ`, all four Penrose equations, the row-space form, left/right
  inverse specializations, zero behavior, a four-equation specification, and
  uniqueness.
- Transported the construction through `Matrix.toEuclideanLin` to arbitrary finite
  rectangular real matrices. Proved all four matrix equations, both canonical
  projector matrices, zero behavior, and an explicit nonidentity theorem when the
  prediction kernel contains a nonzero vector.
- Connected `A A⁺` to T-0009's `subspaceProjectorMatrix`. Proved the complement,
  projection, and rejection column-space formulas (12), (15), and (16).
- For equations (17)--(20), proved the exact identity
  `(I - R R⁺) P = Q Q⁺ P` with `R = (I - Q Q⁺)P`, including the intermediate
  `R R⁺ P = R` theorem. For equation (22), proved that the paper's
  `2I - P P⁺ - Q Q⁺` generator has complement-sum range and that its complementary
  projector represents the intersection.

## Obstacles

Mathlib has no ready Moore--Penrose inverse. Using a spectral or SVD definition would
have introduced unnecessary eigenvalue indexing. The effective-domain equivalence
gave a smaller construction, but equality of subspaces did not rewrite projector
terms directly because their typeclass witnesses are dependent; an extensional
projector-equality lemma resolved that proof-engineering issue.
